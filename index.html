Self Health Advice

General Home Medicine Guide Based on Age Group

Important Disclaimer: This website is an educational project created for general awareness. Always consult a qualified medical professional before taking any medicines.

A temporary increase in average body temperature, usually triggered by a response to an infection.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) Paracetamol Drops or Syrup Dosage must strictly follow weight and pediatric guidelines. Never give Aspirin.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) Paracetamol (500mg or 650mg) Maintain at least 4 to 6 hours between doses; do not exceed daily limits.
Elderly (60+ yrs) Paracetamol (500mg) Consult a doctor if liver or kidney history exists.
Home Care: Rest well, place cool damp cloths on the forehead, and consume plenty of water.

Pain arising from the head or upper neck caused by tension, eye strain, or dehydration.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) Paracetamol Syrup Check if the child has screen strain or skipped meals before giving medicine.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) Ibuprofen (400mg) or Paracetamol (650mg) Take after eating food to prevent stomach acidity.
Elderly (60+ yrs) Paracetamol (500mg) Avoid heavy pain relievers if having high blood pressure.
Home Care: Lie down in a dark, quiet room and drink 1-2 glasses of water.

Burning sensation in the lower chest area caused by acid moving up into the food pipe.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) Pediatric Antacid Suspension Only give under pediatric advice; often caused by food sensitivity.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) Liquid Antacid Gel or Pantoprazole Antacid syrups give immediate relief; Pantoprazole is taken empty stomach in the morning.
Elderly (60+ yrs) Antacid Syrups (Aluminium/Magnesium Hydroxide) Do not take immediately before or after prescription heart medicines.
Home Care: Avoid lying down immediately after eating and sip cold milk or water.

Frequent, loose or watery bowel movements leading quickly to loss of electrolytes.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) WHO-ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) & Zinc Syrup Anti-diarrheal tablets are strictly not recommended for children.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) ORS Packets, Loperamide ORS replaces body minerals; Loperamide is only for non-infectious loose stools.
Elderly (60+ yrs) ORS Packets & Probiotic Capsules Ensure continuous hydration to prevent weakness or fainting.
Home Care: Eat simple foods like curd rice, bananas, and boiled potatoes.

An uneasy feeling in the stomach accompanied by an urge to vomit, commonly due to indigestion or motion sickness.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) Domperidone Oral Drops / Syrup Must be prescribed based on the child's exact age and weight.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) Ondansetron (4mg) or Domperidone (10mg) Best taken 30 minutes before a meal or travel.
Elderly (60+ yrs) Ondansetron (4mg) Watch for mild constipation or headache as a side effect.
Home Care: Suck on small pieces of fresh ginger or lemon, and avoid strong cooking smells.

Pain, scratchiness, or irritation of the throat that often worsens when swallowing.

Age Group Common Medicine Key Note
Children (Under 12 yrs) Warm Salt Water Rinse or Throat Lozenges Do not give hard lozenges to children under 4 years due to choking risk.
Adults (12 to 60 yrs) Antiseptic Lozenges (e.g., Strepsils) or Paracetamol Paracetamol helps if there is associated difficulty or pain while swallowing.
Elderly (60+ yrs) Warm Saline Gargles & Medicated Lozenges Check sugar content of throat lozenges if diabetic.
Home Care: Gargle with warm salt water 3 times a day and drink warm honey-ginger tea.
